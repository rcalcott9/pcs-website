About the evidence

The Scientific treatments tab sums up published studies.

How the summaries are made

  • A computer program finds studies and reads them.
  • Two AI models pull out the facts on their own.
  • A third model checks where they disagree.
  • If a fact can't be settled, it is marked as unsure. It is not guessed.
  • People do not check every summary. Each one says it was made automatically.

How studies are rated

We use a fixed set of rules, written down before we look at any results. The rules use standard methods that medical researchers use around the world.

  • Who it covers. Teens (13 to 17) and adults (18 and over) with symptoms after a concussion. We rate the evidence for 4 to 12 weeks after the injury, and for more than 12 weeks, separately.
  • One result at a time. Each result is one treatment, compared with one thing, for one symptom, at one point in time. We show every result, including ones where the treatment did not help.
  • Like with like. We never mix studies that compared the treatment with different things, such as a fake treatment, normal care, or no treatment.
  • Checking for flaws. Each result is checked for problems in how the study was run, using standard tools (RoB 2 and ROBINS-I). The AI answers set questions about the study. A fixed program, not the AI, turns those answers into the rating.
  • How sure we are. We use a standard system called GRADE. It looks at study flaws, how much studies agree, how big and exact the results are, and whether the people studied match you.
  • Combining studies. We only combine results when there are at least 3 similar studies. With fewer, each study is shown on its own.
  • Money. We flag a study when an author makes money from the treatment. The flag is shown, but it does not change the rating.
  • Side effects. We report harms for each treatment. "No harms reported" is not the same as "no harms happened."

Our ratings can be stricter

Our checks for flaws tend to be stricter than those in published reviews. Two examples:

  • People knew which treatment they got and then rated their own symptoms. Knowing can change how people feel and report.
  • Many people dropped out, so a lot of data is missing.

So a treatment may look less certain here than in other reviews.

How to read a summary

Each summary covers one treatment, for one symptom.

It says what the treatment was compared with. A fake (sham) treatment and normal care answer different questions.

The wording tells you how sure the evidence is:

What the certainty words mean
CertaintyWordingWhat it means
HighThe treatment reduces the symptom.We are confident.
ModerateThe treatment probably reduces the symptom.New studies could change this a little.
LowThe treatment may reduce the symptom.New studies could change this a lot.
Very lowThe evidence is very uncertain.We do not know yet.

Certainty is shown for teens and adults, and for how long ago the injury was. A study of one group may not fit another.

What the summaries don't do

  • They don't tell you which treatment to choose.
  • They don't include forum posts. Those are in the Forum treatments tab.

About the symptom check

  • Your answers are scored with fixed, published rules. No AI is used.
  • Most score cut-offs come from studies of the general public, not of concussion. Treat them as a guide.
  • Some questionnaires are not on the site yet. We are waiting for permission to use them.

This site is for learning. It does not diagnose. It does not replace medical care.
