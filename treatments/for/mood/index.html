Mood, anxiety, and stress

AboutTreatments (9)

Feeling low, worried, or on edge.

This page sums up published research reviews and guidelines. It is not a recommendation. Talk to a clinician before you start any treatment.

Order: stronger evidence first, then lower risk, then lower cost. Every treatment we have for this area is listed.

  1. Sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise

    Low risk$100 to $1,000

    Evidence: Stronger evidence (Moderate certainty (GRADE)).

    Studied in: Mostly teens and young adults after sport concussion, both in the first 2 weeks and with lasting symptoms.

    Why this risk and cost

    Risk: The exercise is set by a graded treadmill or bike test (e.g. the Buffalo Concussion Treadmill Test) to stay below the heart rate that brings on symptoms, and it is scaled back if symptoms worsen. Brief, self-limiting symptom flare-ups during testing and exercise are common and expected. Reviews report no serious adverse events: none were documented across 12 studies in post-concussion syndrome (McIntyre 2020, PMID 32079897), and there were no deaths or hospitalisations in 7 studies of acute mTBI, where one participant's symptoms worsened (Coman 2024, PMID 40959158). Leddy 2023 concluded that prescribed aerobic exercise in the first 2 weeks safely helps recovery. No serious harm specific to this treatment was found, so none is listed. General cardiac or musculoskeletal risks of exercise were not looked up and are not claimed.

    Cost: Reuses the existing EX-SSTAE profile. Assumes the trial protocols: about 6 physical-therapist contacts over 12 weeks (an exercise test, 2 supervised sessions and repeat tests at weeks 3, 6 and 9), with the exercise itself done at home. A shorter 6-week version uses 2 visits plus phone calls. US cash prices are about $100-200 per session at independent clinics ($200-350+ at hospital clinics) and $150-350 for a first evaluation. That makes roughly $650-1,350 for the 12-week course and $250-550 for the shorter one. The typical course falls in $100-1,000, though full hospital-based delivery can go over $1,000. A heart-rate monitor costs extra and was not priced.

    Sources: Vuu, 2022, NeuroRehabilitation; Leddy, 2023, British Journal of Sports Medicine; Langevin, 2020, Medicine and Science in Sports and Exercise

  2. Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT)

    Low riskOver $1,000

    Evidence: Limited evidence (Limited trial evidence).

    • Typical cost over $1,000 without insurance

    Studied in: Adults with lasting post-concussion symptoms.

    Why this risk and cost

    Risk: CBT is a talking therapy with no physical or pharmacological harm route. In the only included trial (McGeary 2022, PMID 35759281, PMC9237802; 8 sessions of CBT for headache vs cognitive processing therapy vs usual care in 193 veterans with posttraumatic headache and PTSD symptoms), adverse events were described as 'minimal and similar across treatment groups'. Most of the 160 adverse event records (changes in headache or musculoskeletal pain, increased stress, depression) were judged not study related. The only events attributed to participation were 2 temporary exacerbations of PTSD symptoms, both in the cognitive processing therapy arm, not the CBT-for-headache arm. Per-arm counts and serious adverse events appear only in a supplement (eTables 6-9) that was not available to us. Outside the trial, a meta-analysis of 56 RCTs of psychological interventions for adult PTSD (Hoppen 2022, PMID 35959698, DOI 10.1192/bjp.2022.111) found harms were generally rare (0-5%). Psychological treatment lowered the risk of deterioration compared with controls, and trauma-focused CBT had fewer serious adverse events than other psychological therapies. Rated 'low' rather than 'minimal' because psychotherapy can cause recognised but uncommon and usually temporary harms: symptom deterioration and short-lived increases in distress. No serious harm caused by CBT could be sourced, so serious_harms is left empty.

    Cost: Assumes the course as delivered in the trial: 8 one-hour individual sessions with a licensed psychologist, plus the intake assessment that is usual in practice. The unit price is the average US private-practice cash rate of $143.26 per session, from 175,083 psychotherapists in a national directory (Zhu 2024, PMID 39301411). 8 x $143 = about $1,150 before any intake fee, so the typical uninsured course falls in the >$1,000 band. The course sits close to the boundary: sliding-scale, community-clinic or lower-fee therapists ($100-125 per session) would bring it under $1,000, while doctoral psychologists in high-cost areas would push it well above. Psychotherapy is normally covered by US health insurance under mental health parity and by the VA for veterans. However, about one-third of private-practice psychotherapists do not accept insurance (Zhu 2024), so many patients still pay out of pocket.

    Sources: Sullivan, 2020, Disabil Rehabil; Chen, 2020, J Psychosom Res; Rytter, 2021, JAMA Netw Open

  3. Collaborative care (care management with psychological and medical support)

    Low riskOver $1,000

    Evidence: Limited evidence (Limited trial evidence).

    • Typical cost over $1,000 without insurance

    Studied in: Teens with symptoms lasting more than a month. No trials in adults.

    Why this risk and cost

    Risk: Collaborative care is a care-delivery model (CBT, care management and psychiatric case-review consultation, with medication only when warranted). In the largest trial after concussion (McCarty 2021, PMID 33635325), serious events (psychiatric hospitalisation, suicide attempts) were similar in both arms and judged unrelated to the intervention by the data safety board. Psychological work can cause transient distress, and any medication started through the psychiatric consultation carries its own drug-specific risks; those belong to the drug, not the care model. Rated low rather than minimal because medication management is part of the package. No serious harm attributable to collaborative care itself could be sourced.

    Cost: Course as delivered in the concussion trial (PMID 33635325): up to 6 months of CBT (mean 8.4 sessions) plus care-management contacts and psychiatrist case conferences. The only published per-person delivery cost for an adolescent collaborative care programme (ROAD trial, Wright 2016, PMID 27654449) was an extra $1,475 per person (2010-2013 dollars) for a 12-month programme. A typical self-pay course is therefore around or above $1,000. In practice, collaborative care (CoCM) billing codes are reimbursed by Medicare, many Medicaid programmes and commercial insurers, so insured patients usually face copays, and the model is available only where a practice has implemented it.

    Sources: Schneider, 2023, Br J Sports Med; Patterson Gentile, 2024, Headache (American Headache Society white paper)

  4. Education and reassurance about recovery

    Minimal riskUnder $100

    Evidence: Very little or conflicting evidence.

    Studied in: Adults seen in the emergency department soon after injury, and some studies in children and teens.

    Why this risk and cost

    Risk: Education is written or verbal information about expected symptoms and recovery (information sheets, with or without telephone or in-person follow-up). No plausible mechanism for serious physical harm; none of the reviewed trials or reviews report adverse effects. The main theoretical downside is that poorly framed information could heighten symptom worry, which is why guidelines emphasise reassurance about a good expected recovery.

    Cost: Course: an information sheet or brief verbal explanation given at the emergency department or clinic visit (the interventions in the Eliyahu 2016 review), optionally with a follow-up phone call. This is delivered as part of a visit the patient is already having, and patient leaflets are typically free; no separate charge is expected beyond the index visit.

    Sources: Eliyahu, 2016, Acad Emerg Med; Teo, 2020, Brain Inj; Silverberg, 2025, Ann Fam Med (Action Collaborative on TBI Care guideline)

  5. Acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT)

    Minimal risk$100 to $1,000

    Evidence: Very little or conflicting evidence.

    Studied in: Brain injury of all severities, mostly moderate to severe. One small study in mild TBI.

    Why this risk and cost

    Risk: ACT is a talk therapy (typically about five 1-hour sessions with a psychologist: acceptance, defusion, values and committed-action exercises plus homework). It involves no drug, device or physical load, so there is no plausible mechanism for serious harm; the expected downside is transient emotional discomfort during experiential exercises. The mTBI feasibility study (PMID 39956598) did not report adverse-event data, and the Cochrane review of psychological therapies for chronic pain (Williams 2020, PMID 32794606) found adverse events poorly reported for ACT but identified no serious harms attributable to it.

    Cost: Course: 5 one-hour individual sessions (as in ACTion-mTBI). Mean US private-pay psychotherapy rate about $143/session (Zhu et al. 2024, PMID 39301411), so about $716 per course; higher-priced doctoral psychologists may push this toward $1,000.

    Sources: Penubarthi, 2025, Indian J Psychol Med; Faulkner (ACTion-mTBI), 2025, BMJ Open

  6. Multidisciplinary or interdisciplinary rehabilitation

    Minimal risk$100 to $1,000

    Evidence: Very little or conflicting evidence (“Very low and low levels of evidence” (source wording); weak recommendation).

    Studied in: Adults with lasting post-concussion symptoms.

    Why this risk and cost

    Risk: Coordinated programmes combine education, CBT-based psychological support, graded activity/exercise and therapy from several professions; no drugs, devices or procedures. In the GAIN trials (reused cost/risk profile MULTI-GAIN), the only harm recorded was minor, temporary fatigue or symptom increase (7/148 adults), with no serious adverse events; the adolescent trial reported none. Harms data are thin (two trials, passive collection in one). No sourced serious harm was found.

    Cost: Reused from the MULTI-GAIN profile. Course assumption: a trial-style programme of 1 evaluation, about 3 group and 5 individual sessions over 8 weeks. At 2026 Medicare national non-facility rates (CPT 97162 about $98; 97110 about $29 per 15-minute unit; 90853 about $30 per group session), the total is roughly $700. Self-pay rates are usually higher than Medicare (an unsourced assumption), and broader US multidisciplinary clinic programmes with more sessions or neuropsychological testing can exceed $1,000, so the estimate sits near the upper boundary.

    Sources: Rytter, 2021, JAMA Netw Open (Danish national guideline, GRADE); Möller, 2021, J Rehabil Med; Moore, 2024, Disabil Rehabil

  7. Antidepressant medication (SSRIs such as sertraline or citalopram; SNRIs) for depression or anxiety

    Moderate risk$100 to $1,000

    Evidence: Very little or conflicting evidence.

    • Moderate risk: Suicidal thoughts and behaviours in children, adolescents and young adults (boxed warning); Serotonin syndrome, alone or with other serotonergic drugs; Increased bleeding risk, especially with aspirin, NSAIDs or anticoagulants; Hyponatremia (low blood sodium), with reported sodium below 110 mmol/L; Activation of mania or hypomania; seizures (use with caution in seizure disorders)

    Studied in: Adults with depression or anxiety after TBI, mostly not mild; very few mild-TBI trials

    Why this risk and cost

    Risk: Prescription-only. Common side effects (nausea, diarrhoea, dizziness, dry mouth, sexual dysfunction) are usually tolerable, but in a TBI network meta-analysis sertraline had a higher dropout rate than placebo (OR 2.65; Cheng 2021, PMID 33497170). The FDA label carries a boxed warning for suicidal thoughts and behaviours in children and young adults and requires monitoring for clinical worsening, screening for bipolar disorder, and caution with seizure disorders, which matters after brain injury. Serotonin syndrome, bleeding (especially with NSAIDs or anticoagulants) and hyponatremia are recognised serious harms. Stopping abruptly can cause discontinuation symptoms. Because medical screening and follow-up are required, it rates moderate.

    Cost: Course assumption: 6 months of generic sertraline 50 mg once daily (about 180 tablets), with one new-patient prescriber visit and two follow-up visits. Drug: NADAC (effective 2025-12-17) is $0.03876 per 50 mg tablet, so 180 tablets cost about $7 at acquisition cost; retail cash prices are higher but generic SSRIs are typically well under $100 for the course. Visits at CMS 2026 Physician Fee Schedule national non-facility rates (conversion factor $33.4009): 99204 new patient, 5.31 RVU = $177.36; 99214 established patient, 4.06 RVU = $135.61 each. That gives about $450 in visits and about $460-550 for the course at Medicare rates. Uninsured cash visit prices are often higher, but the course usually stays in the $100-1,000 band. The medication alone is under $100.

    Sources: Feinberg C, 2021, JAMA Neurol (systematic review of drug treatments in mild TBI; sertraline among the few drugs studied more than once); Hicks AJ, 2021, J Neurotrauma (systematic review of drug treatments for anxiety after TBI; only trial found was citalopram in mild TBI); Kreitzer N, 2019, J Head Trauma Rehabil (meta-analysis of antidepressants for major depression after TBI of any severity)

  8. Hyperbaric oxygen therapy

    Moderate riskOver $1,000

    Evidence: Very little or conflicting evidence.

    • Moderate risk: ear barotrauma in about 15% of patients, usually temporary; serious harms such as seizures are rare.
    • Typical cost over $1,000 without insurance

    Studied in: Adults, mostly with mild TBI.

    Why this risk and cost

    Risk: Treatment requires medical screening (ear equalisation, seizure history, lung disease) and chamber supervision. Middle-ear barotrauma is common (otologic adverse events in 15% of 18,284 patients across 54 studies; 6.4% of barotrauma episodes severe) and ear pain is a frequent reason to stop. Oxygen-toxicity seizures are serious but rare (about 0.05% of sessions in a 10,425-session registry), with higher risk in people with a seizure history. The 2026 meta-analysis reported most adverse events in TBI trials were mild and transient, and the 1.5 ATA dose used in concussion trials is lower than the 2.0-2.4 ATA in much of the harms data. The earlier pipeline profile rated this 'high' to match a rubric example; on this page's definitions (serious harm at a non-trivial rate, or supervision/screening needed) it fits 'moderate'.

    Cost: Course as used in concussion trials: about 40 sessions of 60 minutes at 1.5 ATA. US self-pay prices run from about $100 per session at freestanding clinics to over $1,000 at hospitals, so even the cheapest course (40 x $100 = $4,000) is well above $1,000; a typical $150-$250 per session gives $6,000-$10,000. Medicare NCD 20.29 does not list concussion/TBI as a covered indication, and commercial insurers generally follow similar lists, so this is usually paid out of pocket. Soft-sided 'mild' chambers (about 1.3 ATA) cost less but do not deliver the trial dose.

    Sources: Wang, 2026, Complement Ther Med; Crawford, 2017, J Head Trauma Rehabil; Harch, 2022, Front Neurol

Treatments for specific lasting symptoms

Some lasting symptoms, like migraine-type headache or poor sleep, also happen in other conditions. These treatments were studied mostly in people with those conditions, not after a concussion. Each one says what is known after concussion.

  1. SSRI or SNRI antidepressants for anxiety

    Moderate risk$100 to $1,000

    Evidence in generalised anxiety disorder: Stronger evidence (High certainty (Cochrane review, GRADE)). Needs a prescription.

    • Moderate risk: Suicidal thoughts and behaviour in children and young adults (FDA boxed warning); Serotonin syndrome, especially with other serotonergic drugs such as triptans; Increased bleeding risk with NSAIDs, aspirin, antiplatelets or anticoagulants; hyponatremia (low sodium); Discontinuation symptoms if stopped abruptly; raised blood pressure with venlafaxine; Stopping because of side effects: RR 2.18 versus placebo (number needed to harm 17)

    Studied in: Adults with generalised anxiety disorder, not specifically after concussion

    After concussion: One small trial of citalopram (19 service members with mild TBI, stopped early) found no effect on anxiety

    Why this risk and cost

    Risk: Prescription drugs that need a clinician to start, adjust and monitor them, and to taper them when stopping. In the 2025 Cochrane review, side effects such as nausea, sleep change or sexual problems made about 1 in 17 more people stop than on placebo (RR 2.18, high certainty), though overall drop-out was similar to placebo. All antidepressants carry an FDA boxed warning for increased suicidal thoughts and behaviour in children and young adults. Labels list rarer serious harms: serotonin syndrome (especially combined with other serotonergic drugs such as triptans), bleeding risk with NSAIDs, aspirin or anticoagulants, low sodium (mainly in older adults), angle-closure glaucoma, and raised blood pressure with venlafaxine.

    Cost: Course assumption: generic escitalopram or sertraline (or venlafaxine XR / duloxetine) taken for about 9-12 months, since guidelines advise continuing 6-12 months after remission, with about three prescriber visits. The drugs are cheap generics: CMS NADAC per unit is about $0.04 for escitalopram 10 mg and sertraline 50 mg, $0.08 for venlafaxine ER 75 mg and $0.14 for duloxetine 60 mg, i.e. roughly $15-50 a year at acquisition cost; cash pharmacy prices are higher but typically under $100 a year for generic SSRIs. Three office visits (CPT 99214, 2026 Medicare non-facility about $136 each; visit figure reused from the existing topiramate-migraine cost profile) add about $400, so about $420-500 in total. The medication alone is under $100.

    Sources: Kopcalic K, 2025, Cochrane Database Syst Rev; Bandelow B, 2022, Eur Arch Psychiatry Clin Neurosci (German anxiety disorders guideline)
