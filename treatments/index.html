Treatments

This list summarizes published research reviews and guidelines. It is not a recommendation. Talk to a clinician before you start any treatment.

This list isn't complete. Some treatments people try aren't here because published reviews haven't covered them yet.

How the list is ordered: treatments with stronger research evidence come first. Within each level, lower-risk and then lower-cost treatments come first. Every treatment stays on the list. A warning says why if a treatment has moderate or high risk, or usually costs over $1,000.

Each row says who the treatment was studied in. Cost is the typical cost in the US without insurance. You can sort by any column.

  • Moderate risk: Amitriptyline (tricyclic): boxed warning - increased suicidal thinking and behaviour in children, adolescents and young adults; Amitriptyline: arrhythmias, conduction prolongation; myocardial infarction and stroke reported with the class; can precipitate angle-closure glaucoma; Topiramate: acute myopia and secondary angle-closure glaucoma (possible permanent vision loss); metabolic acidosis; Topiramate: fetal toxicity (cleft lip/palate), suicidal ideation, cognitive/neuropsychiatric effects, serious skin reactions; Erenumab (anti-CGRP): constipation with serious complications (hospitalisation, surgery), new or worsening hypertension, hypersensitivity incl. anaphylaxis, Raynaud's phenomenon

Studied in: Adults (civilian and military); timing not clearly reported in the review abstract

 Headache Very little or conflicting evidence Very little or conflicting evidence
What the source says
“Pharmacological treatments for PTH may provide improvements in headache frequency and intensity; however, evidence for their efficacy is limited and inconsistent. Given the limited high-quality evidence overall, no specific clinical recommendations can be made at this time.”

Hyperbaric oxygen therapy
  • Moderate risk: ear barotrauma in about 15% of patients, usually temporary; serious harms such as seizures are rare.PMID 41429031, PMID 42575227, PMID 33648032

Studied in: Adults, mostly mild TBI; time since injury not reported in the review abstract

Trials comparing it with a sham (fake) treatment in mild TBI found little difference from sham. PMID 27603765

What the source says
“Although growing evidence suggests potential benefits of Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy (HBOT) for TBI, its efficacy remains controversial.”
