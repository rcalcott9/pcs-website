Treatments

This list sums up published research reviews and guidelines. It is not a recommendation. Talk to a clinician before you start any treatment.

Lower-risk treatments come first. Within each risk level, treatments that studies suggest may help come next, then mixed results, then little or no benefit; then lower cost. A warning explains any treatment with moderate or high risk, or a typical cost over $1,000.

Each row says who the treatment was studied in. Costs are typical US prices for a full course, without insurance.

Most concussion treatments have only small studies so far. “Early studies” means we can’t be sure yet. It doesn’t mean the treatment doesn’t work.

The list isn’t complete. Some treatments haven’t been covered by published reviews yet.

Melatonin

Studied in: Children and teens aged 8-18, 4 to 6 weeks after concussion (one trial)

Sleep: in 72 children and teens with sleep problems after concussion, melatonin improved some sleep measures, such as sleep time, over 2 weeks. This was a secondary analysis of one trial. PMID 32988292

Overall recovery: in the main trial of 99 children and teens, melatonin did not improve overall concussion symptoms more than a placebo after 4 weeks. PMID 32217739

 Sleep Early studies: may help Some sleep measures improved; overall recovery did not. Our check: very low certainty (GRADE) Source review: Very little or conflicting evidence
